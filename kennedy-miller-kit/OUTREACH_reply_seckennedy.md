## B. Threaded version

**1/** Two things are true at once here, and the corpus keeps them apart.

**2/** Miller's paper read a cluster of infant deaths in the days after vaccination as a
SIDS signal. As *method*, that inference fails: VAERS is passive surveillance with **no
denominator** — you never know how many vaccinated infants didn't die — so a count of
deaths-after-shots can't become a rate, and can't be compared to background mortality.

**3/** It gets sharper. We ran Miller's own metric on the full record (VAERS 1990–2026,
n=1.99M) with controls side by side. The day-0–2 clustering is **stronger in all-infant
reports — most of them non-fatal** — than in infant deaths, and it's strong across the
**entire database**. A causal hazard can't explain why trivial reports cluster *harder*
against the shot date. Reporting behaviour can. HHS's data guide says exactly this.

**4/** So the clustering is a property of how the system *records*, not of how vaccines
*harm*. That's not a pro- or anti-vaccine statement — it's the instrument telling you it
can't answer this question. The place to answer it is a denominated cohort / self-controlled
design, not the spontaneous-report pile.

**5/** And the other half: the **removal** has to meet the same bar. COPE guidance — which
the Secretary's letter rightly cites — says a retraction notice must state its reasons. A
two-sentence notice asserting "conclusions not supported by the methodology," without
publishing the analysis, naming the reviewers, or disclosing their conflicts, *asserts* the
flaw instead of *demonstrating* it. The transparency demand is well-formed.

**6/** You can hold both: the inference was invalid **and** the erasure should have shown its
work. Same principle generates both. We don't ask the public to accept either behind closed
doors.

**7/** Everything here is open and hash-verified — retracted paper retained (not deleted),
HHS data guide, COPE guidance, the letter, and the sample pull, each traceable to a primary
source with a recomputable SHA-256. Retracted ≠ deleted. Lore ≠ evidence. Method ≠ proof.

*Leads, not verdicts. Not medical advice.*

## C. If asked "so which side are you on?"

On method, not party. Passive surveillance can't prove the link Miller claimed **or** the
safety its critics imply — it has no denominator. We publish the comparison that *has* one,
label the one that doesn't as reporting-behaviour, and insist a removal show its work the
same way a paper must. That's the whole position.
