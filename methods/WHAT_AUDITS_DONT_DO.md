# What Audits Don't Do

> *What audits don't do is more important than what they do.*

## A Statement of Method and Limits

We build open, reproducible tools for reading the public adverse-event record — VAERS, V-Safe, FAERS, and their counterparts in other countries — and we hold the systems that produce that record to an honest standard. We are not the custodian of anyone's data and we are not the arbiter of anyone's safety. We are a transparent instrument and, where the evidence earns it, a strong critic and a form of guidance.

One day there may be a perfect database: complete, denominated, actively ascertained, privacy-preserving, and open to independent analysis. Until that day exists, the honest move is not to pretend we have it. It is to work in the open with what is available, to say plainly what the available data can and cannot support, and to keep asking — of every system, including our own — *what can this instrument not see, and why.*

## What we claim, and what we do not

We claim **transparency, not proof.** Specifically, we claim proof of source, proof of path, and proof of who / what / where / when / how, wherever those can be established mathematically — a hash anyone can recompute, a pipeline anyone can re-run, a number anyone can trace back to its government source byte-for-byte.

We do **not** claim to have proven that any vaccine, or anything else, is safe or unsafe. Passive and self-reported systems record temporal association, not causation. No amount of clean processing changes what the underlying instrument was built to capture. We will not let the reproducibility of our method be mistaken for proof of a conclusion. Those are two different things, and we keep them apart.

## The test you can hold us to

This is our standing commitment. It is a test, and you are invited to apply it to us:

1. **Findings carry a real denominator and matched ascertainment.** When we publish a comparison as a finding, it comes with a denominator and a like-for-like comparison group. Re-run it. Attack the method. It holds, or we retract it.
2. **No denominator-free cross-system comparison is ever published as a finding.** When we use such a comparison at all, it is an internal disproportionality diagnostic, labeled as reporting-behavior, never as risk.
3. **Every number ships with its limits attached.** Counts that are a floor are named as a floor. The absence of a denominator is stated. Ascertainment differences are disclosed. The method travels with the number so anyone can check it.

## Silent is not negative

A surveillance instrument with a closed vocabulary cannot report what it has no field for. When such a system is quiet about an outcome it was never built to capture, that silence is **not** a negative finding — it is a blind spot. Reading the absence of a field as the absence of an event is the central error we refuse to make.

So we name our blind spots, and we name theirs. An audit that knows where not to look, and looks away, is not an audit — it is a whitewash. We would rather publish a smaller, honest picture that declares what it cannot see than a larger one that hides its own edges.

## A tool, not a verdict

Many real decisions are bets made under uncertainty — a treatment whose potential may be worth its risk, with no clean evaluation to settle the question. Our job is to make the odds legible, not to call the bet for anyone. The same engine that can surface a possible harm signal can surface a possible benefit signal, and we hold both to the same standard. The direction we point the tool is not a thesis; the discipline we apply is.

## Open, self-correcting, and yours to verify

Our path is open so that others can walk it. Anyone can re-derive our artifacts, confirm the path is valid, and contribute their own. When the science changes, we change with it; when we are wrong, the record shows the correction rather than hiding it. This is the one advantage that a transparent, crowd-verifiable method has over a closed one: it can be checked, challenged, and improved by people who owe us nothing.

## On the systems we read

Some of the gaps we find are structural and documented. A legal duty to report adverse events that carries no enforcement and no penalty produces predictable under-reporting. An active-monitoring tool built around reactogenicity is, by design, thin on serious events. Data fragmented across systems that cannot be analyzed together impedes the integrated picture — and privacy protection does not require that fragmentation, because privacy-preserving integration is technically possible.

We name these as effects, not as motives. We do not need to allege that any hole was dug on purpose; it is enough that the hole is real, that it weakens what the public can know, and that privacy alone does not explain it. To the institutions that maintain these systems, our question is simple and answerable: *the gap is not required by privacy — so what is the reason for it?* If there is a good reason, it should be easy to give.

## Why this matters

We take seriously the warning in Virginia Eubanks's *Automating Inequality* — that automated systems, run at speed and scale and hidden behind complexity, can profile, police, and punish the most vulnerable people while appearing neutral. That warning is exactly why our discipline is transparency, declared limits, open method, and the refusal to overclaim. Automation aimed at the public good is only as trustworthy as it is auditable. Used in the open, with humility, to hold public-health data systems accountable and to put a clear, checkable picture in ordinary people's hands, artificial intelligence in service of this problem is a goal worth the discipline it demands.

Until the perfect database exists, we will be a transparent tool, a strong critic, and a form of guidance — and we will hold ourselves to the same test we ask of everyone else.

## How to hold us to this

Re-run our queries. Recompute our hashes. Attack our method. Tell us where an envelope re-identifies someone, where a number lacks the limits it should carry, where our own audit looked away. The method is public for exactly this reason. Bring the scrutiny — it makes the work stronger.

*This is a statement of method, not a claim of findings. It takes no position on whether any vaccine is safe or unsafe. It commits only to how we work, what we will and will not say, and the standard to which we invite you to hold us.*
